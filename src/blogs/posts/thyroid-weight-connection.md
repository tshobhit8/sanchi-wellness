## The Thyroid-Weight Connection Explained

Your thyroid regulates metabolism, the rate at which your body converts food into energy. When it's underactive (Hypothyroidism), metabolism slows down, which can lead to weight gain even without changes to diet or activity. When it's overactive (Hyperthyroidism), the opposite can happen. This is why thyroid conditions are often first noticed through unexplained weight changes, fatigue, or difficulty losing weight despite effort.

</div>

<div id="signs">

## Signs Your Thyroid Might Be Affecting Your Weight

Common signs of Hypothyroidism include unexplained weight gain, persistent fatigue, feeling cold often, dry skin, hair thinning, and constipation. If you're experiencing several of these alongside weight that won't shift despite reasonable diet and exercise, it's worth getting your TSH levels checked with a doctor. Nutrition can support thyroid health, but a proper diagnosis always comes first.

</div>

<div id="nutrition">

## Nutrition Strategies for Hypothyroidism

The goal isn't a restrictive diet, it's a nutrient-dense one that supports metabolism and energy. Prioritize protein at each meal to support muscle mass and metabolic rate, include fiber-rich vegetables and whole grains to manage the constipation common with Hypothyroidism, and stay consistent with meal timing to help regulate energy levels throughout the day.

</div>

<div id="nutrients">

## Foods and Nutrients That Support Thyroid Function

Iodine (found in dairy and iodized salt), selenium (found in nuts like Brazil nuts, though moderation matters), zinc, and vitamin D all play roles in thyroid hormone production. Cruciferous vegetables like cabbage and cauliflower are nutritious but contain goitrogens, compounds that can interfere with iodine uptake in very large raw quantities. Cooking them (rather than eating them raw in excess) reduces this effect, so there's no need to avoid them.

</div>

<div id="medication">

## Working With Your Medication, Not Against It

If you're on thyroid medication like levothyroxine, timing matters. It's typically taken on an empty stomach, and calcium or iron-rich foods and supplements can interfere with absorption if taken too close together. A good nutrition plan works around your medication schedule rather than ignoring it, which is one of the most overlooked factors in managing thyroid-related weight changes effectively.

</div>
